## Consequences for the FPGA design (all [FPGA-APPROX])

1. One p-bit = **MAC over ±J (XOR sign) + bias → sigmoid → compare with noise → latch**. This mirrors Fig. 10 of DTM [DTM, p.25]. The digital MAC needs no multiplier because x_j ∈ {−1,+1}: each term is +J or −J, which is the digital equivalent of the XOR sign-correction y = x ⊕ s.
2. The RNG becomes an LFSR (pseudo-random uniform word). The Gaussian-comparator physics [DTM, p.37] becomes `rand < sigmoid(logit)`, an inverse-CDF comparison. The two give the same Bernoulli law when the LUT holds the exact target probability.
3. Chromatic (2-colour) scheduling as in DTM/torx. Our FPGA time-multiplexes the p-bit cell instead of having one physical cell per node.
4. β is handled as in thrml/03: a schedule multiplies the local field before the sigmoid.
5. The denoising demo is **feasible** at toy scale. DTM gives the forward coupling Γ(t)/2 and the clamped x^t nodes. It does **not** give trained weights for small grids, so the marginal-energy weights in our demo are hand-set (Hebbian pattern storage). That is an [FPGA-APPROX] choice, not Extropic's model.
