Thermal Computer on a Tang Nano
Reverse-engineered from Extropic's papers · built in Verilog · Tang Nano 9K

A computer made of biased coins

A thermodynamic computer does not compute answers. It samples them. Its gate is the p-bit, a bit that flips at random with odds you program. Wire p-bits together with couplings J and biases h, let them flip, and the states they visit most are the low-energy ones, which is where the answers are. This page shows each basic unit, how the units connect, and how this repo runs them on a $20 FPGA.

1 · the basic units

Every classical block has a thermal twin

In a CPU, noise is the enemy and every block is deterministic. In a thermodynamic computer, noise is the fuel and every block is a sampler. All the right-hand units are built from one primitive, the p-bit.

2 · the p-bit

One p-bit: weigh the neighbours, add noise, compare, latch

The neighbours' states, weighted by J, plus the bias h give a field. The field shifts noise in front of a comparator, so P(1) = σ(2β·field) [DTM p.5 Eq. 11]. β is the inverse temperature: high β means sharp, almost deterministic; low β means hot and random.

3 · p-gates

AND, OR, NOT as energy landscapes, and they run backwards

Pick J and h so that the truth-table rows are the lowest-energy states. A free-running p-gate then visits only (almost only) valid rows. Clamp a p-bit (pin it with a huge bias) to choose the direction: clamp the inputs for a normal gate, or clamp the output and watch it find inputs that produce it.

4 · connecting units

Wire gates into a multiplier, clamp the product, get the factors

A wire between two gates is one shared p-bit, and the circuit's energy is the sum of the gates' energies. Six p-ANDs and two p-XORs make a 2×2-bit multiplier of 14 p-bits. Clamp the product bits and the sampler hunts for a and b. This is the same hardware and the same J; only four bias words change.

5 · programs as energies

Annealing: start hot, cool slowly, land in a deep valley

Max-Cut on the built-in 16-node graph (case maxcut16, the board's power-on program). Antiferromagnetic couplings J = −1 make neighbours want to disagree, so a low energy means many cut edges. The schedule raises β from 0.25 to 4. The optimum cut is 20 of 24 edges.

β = 0.25 · cut = 0 / 24 · best = 0 (optimum 20)

Cut size over sweeps

The RTL schedule is 16 β steps × 8 sweeps. In the Python mirror of the bit-exact RTL, 96 % of anneals reach the optimum (docs/06).

6 · on the board

The whole machine on the Tang Nano 9K

Orange is the thermodynamic core. Everything around it is plain digital plumbing that plays the role of Extropic's XTR-0 host + FPGA. No DSP blocks are used; the p-bit engine is shifts, adds and a table.

Run it

source ~/tools/oss-cad-suite/environment
cd tb  && make test     # bit-exact RTL vs model
make pgates             # gates fwd/bwd, factoring
cd ../fpga && make      # default bitstream
make case CASE=factor6  # power-on = factor 6
make flash-case         # program the board
python3 host.py pgate --case pgate_and --clamp Y=1

What the FPGA does not capture

The noise is a deterministic LFSR, not shot noise; one engine visits p-bits in turn instead of thousands of cells flipping in parallel; there is no analog relaxation, device variation or energy saving. It reproduces the mathematics of the chip exactly, not its physics (docs/08).

Bitstreams were built and timed; flashing and host.py were not run because no board was attached.
